Saudi company setup in 2 stages: your foreign company first, then the MISA licence

20 Oct 2026 · 3 min read

Foreign investors set up in Saudi Arabia through a foreign company. Here's what each stage involves, what it costs, how long it takes and what you need to provide.

A foreign investor doesn't usually set up in Saudi Arabia as a private person. The Saudi company is owned by a foreign company, and that company's documents go into the application. So the work comes in two stages: first the foreign company, then the Saudi company itself.

Knowing this from the start saves time. Many investors begin with the Saudi paperwork and only then find out that their foreign company, its statements and its attested documents are the first thing they need.

Stage 1: your foreign company

This is the company that will own your Saudi company. Many investors want one with a track record rather than one formed last week, so we offer established companies with at least one year of history:

  • Pakistan: RM 7,999, about 6 weeks.
  • Egypt: RM 9,999, 3 to 4 weeks. Financial statements not included.
  • Malaysia: RM 11,999, 2 to 3 weeks.
  • United Kingdom: RM 13,999, 2 to 3 weeks.

Each includes the ownership transfer to you, a name change if needed, full attestation of the documents and courier of the originals to your country. Malaysia, the UK and Pakistan also include last-year financial statements. Government processing and public holidays can add time.

Stage 2: your Saudi company

Once the foreign company is ready, we take it through the Saudi side. Our Saudi company setup starts from RM 12,999 and covers:

  • File review. We check your foreign company's documents and your passport before anything is submitted.
  • Attestation and power of attorney. Your company documents are attested by the Saudi embassy, and a legal power of attorney is issued for the application.
  • Investment licence. We prepare your application to the Ministry of Investment (MISA) and follow it until a decision is made.
  • Commercial registration. Issued through the Saudi Business Center, with the articles of association.
  • Government platforms. Your company's accounts are activated on Qiwa, Mudad, Muqeem and GOSI, so you can hire and operate.

This stage usually takes 15 to 30 days, counted from the day your file is complete and the government fees are paid. The timeline pauses while documents or payments are pending.

What you need to provide

  • A foreign company that will own the Saudi company (Stage 1)
  • Audited financial statements of the foreign company
  • Company documents attested by the Saudi embassy
  • A legal power of attorney for the application
  • The investor's passport copy
  • Payment of the government fees when they are issued

What the price doesn't include

Government, licence and registration fees, medical insurance, passport and platform fees, Saudi embassy attestation fees, capital and VAT are paid separately at actual cost through SADAD, with the official receipts. Work permits and expat levies are also separate.

What to keep in mind

  • The Saudi authorities decide every licence, and their requirements can change. Nobody can guarantee approval.
  • An established company can support your application, but it doesn't guarantee a licence, a bank account or any other approval.
  • Setting up a company doesn't give you a visa or residency by itself.

Each stage is invoiced separately, with a written agreement, and Stage 2 starts once Stage 1 is complete.

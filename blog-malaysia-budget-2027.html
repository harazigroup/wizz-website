Malaysia Budget 2027: what changes for companies and business owners

14 Oct 2026 · 3 min read

Budget 2027 cuts tax for small companies, raises the minimum wage and adds new financing. Here is what changes, and what it means if you own a Malaysian company from abroad.

Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim tabled Budget 2027 on 9 October 2026. These are the measures that matter most for companies. The tax changes are proposals until Parliament passes the Finance Bill, so details can still change.

1. Lower income tax for small companies

From year of assessment 2027, the proposed rates for micro, small and medium companies are:

  • 14% on the first RM150,000 of chargeable income (down from 15%)
  • 16% on RM150,001 to RM600,000 (down from 17%)
  • 24% on income above RM600,000 (unchanged)

The government expects around 300,000 businesses to save up to RM6,000 each.

Who gets the lower rates

The company needs paid-up capital of RM2.5 million or less and annual sales of up to RM50 million. It must also be at least 80% owned by Malaysian companies or Malaysian citizens. A Sdn. Bhd. owned mostly by foreign shareholders does not get these SME rates and pays the standard 24%. Check your own position with a qualified tax adviser.

2. Bigger write-offs for equipment

  • Small-value assets: the limit for a full write-off rises from RM2,000 to RM3,000 per asset
  • Accelerated capital allowances for ICT equipment, software and locally made machinery are extended to 31 December 2030

3. Stamp duty savings

  • Opening a savings or current account: fully exempt from 1 January 2027
  • Foreign-currency loans from Malaysian banks: stamp duty capped at RM2,000 (up to RM100 million) or RM5,000 (above that), from 1 January 2027
  • Peer-to-peer financing for small businesses: capped at RM10 from 2027 to 2030

4. Higher minimum wage

The minimum wage rises from RM1,700 to RM2,000 a month from June 2027. Businesses with annual sales below RM50 million are exempt for now; how long the exemption lasts has not been announced. A separate starting salary of RM2,500 a month applies to graduates and semi-skilled jobs.

5. Financing and export support

  • RM57 billion in financing and guarantees for businesses
  • RM1 billion through BPMB for small businesses entering export markets
  • RM60 million for MATRADE market-entry programmes
  • RM30 million through MDEC to help 4,000 small businesses adopt AI and automation

6. Regional hub companies

Groups that run regional services from Malaysia get an improved Global Services Hub incentive from 1 January 2027, with a 5% tax rate for qualifying new companies.

What did not change

No changes to e-invoicing were announced, and the rollout continues. If your company issues invoices in Malaysia, keep preparing for it.

What this means if you are setting up in Malaysia

Ownership now matters even more. A company owned at least 80% by Malaysians can use the lower SME rates; a foreign-owned company usually cannot, even with the same profits. Plan the shareholding before you incorporate, not after.

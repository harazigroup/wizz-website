Adding a partner to your US LLC: what changes for non-resident owners

15 Oct 2026 · 2 min read

Bringing a second owner into your LLC is more than a signature. Your tax filings change, your bank and payment accounts need updating, and some states need a filing. Here is the checklist.

Many founders start a US LLC alone and bring in a partner later: a co-founder, an investor or a family member. Adding a member is allowed, but it changes how the company is treated, especially when the owners live outside the US.

1. Your tax filings change

A single-member LLC owned by a non-resident is a "disregarded entity". Each year it files Form 5472 with a pro-forma Form 1120.

Once there are two or more members, the LLC is normally treated as a partnership, unless it has chosen to be taxed as a corporation. That means:

  • Form 1065 (the partnership return) every year, due 15 March for calendar-year companies
  • A Schedule K-1 for each member, showing their share of the profit or loss
  • For foreign members, possible withholding on US business income, reported on Forms 8804 and 8805
  • Form 5472 usually no longer applies

The partnership usually needs its own EIN, separate from the one your single-member LLC used. Ask a qualified US tax adviser before the change takes effect, not after.

2. Agree the terms and update the operating agreement

Write down the new member's ownership percentage, what they contribute, how profits are shared, who makes decisions and what happens if someone leaves. Then sign an amended operating agreement. This is the document banks and payment providers will ask for.

3. Check your state

Some states want a filing when members change; others keep ownership off the public record. In New Mexico and Wyoming, members are usually not listed in state filings, so often nothing needs filing with the state. If your LLC is registered in other states too, check each one.

4. Tell the IRS

Get the EIN the partnership needs. If the new member becomes the company's "responsible party", file Form 8822-B within 60 days.

5. Update banks and payment providers

Stripe, PayPal, Wise, WorldFirst and banks verify every significant owner, usually anyone with 25% or more. Your new partner will need to complete identity checks, and a new EIN can mean updating or reopening accounts. Expect a review, and plan for it so payments don't stop.

Quick checklist

  1. Agree ownership and terms in writing
  2. Sign the amended operating agreement
  3. Check state filing requirements
  4. Get the right EIN and update the IRS if needed
  5. Update bank and payment accounts
  6. Switch your yearly filings from Form 5472 to Form 1065

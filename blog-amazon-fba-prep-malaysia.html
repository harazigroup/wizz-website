Amazon FBA prep from Malaysia: how it works, step by step

21 Oct 2026 · 3 min read

With Fulfilment by Amazon, Amazon stores your stock and delivers your orders. First, your products must reach Amazon checked, labelled and packed the way Amazon asks. Here's how that works from Malaysia.

Fulfilment by Amazon (FBA) lets you keep your products in Amazon's warehouses. When a customer orders, Amazon picks, packs and delivers the item. For many sellers it is the simplest way to sell in the US, the UK, Canada and Europe without running a warehouse there.

The work happens before that. Your stock has to arrive prepared exactly the way Amazon asks. Units with missing labels or the wrong packaging can be delayed, charged extra or refused. Getting this right is what "FBA prep" means.

What you need before you ship

  • A seller account on the Amazon marketplace you want, with company details that match your documents.
  • Products you are allowed to sell there. Some categories need Amazon's approval, and some products can't be sent to FBA at all.
  • A listing for each product, so Amazon can issue its product labels.
  • A shipment plan in Seller Central. Amazon decides which fulfilment centre receives your stock.

Step 1: Receive

Your supplier sends the products to us in Malaysia, or we source them for you in Malaysia.

Step 2: Inspect and count

We check the quantity and visible condition against your order. If something is missing or damaged, you hear about it while the stock is still in Malaysia, not after it reaches Amazon.

Step 3: Label and pack

We apply FNSKU labels, the barcodes Amazon uses to identify your units, and bag, bundle and box the products as needed. Each carton gets its own carton label.

Step 4: Ship to Amazon

We send the shipment by air or sea freight to the fulfilment centre Amazon assigns. Air is usually faster; sea usually costs less for larger shipments. We also coordinate the customs paperwork.

Step 5: Track

You get updates until Amazon checks the shipment in. Once it is received, the stock shows in your Amazon inventory.

What to keep in mind

  • Amazon makes the final decision on receiving inventory. Shipments must meet Amazon's prep, labelling and product rules, and those rules change.
  • Amazon approves seller accounts and restricted categories. No service can guarantee approval.
  • Import duties and taxes in the destination country depend on your products and set-up. Ask a qualified customs broker or tax adviser.

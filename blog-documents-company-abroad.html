What documents do you need to open a company abroad?

26 Oct 2026 · 3 min read

For most countries the core list is short: a passport copy and your full details, written exactly as on your documents. Here is what is usually asked for, where some countries ask for more, and how to avoid delays.

Opening a company in another country is mostly paperwork, and most delays come from documents that are unclear or don't match. The good news is that for many countries the list is shorter than people expect.

Here is what is usually needed, and where some countries ask for more.

1. A passport copy for every owner and director

The passport is the core document almost everywhere. We need a clear colour copy of the photo page for each director and each shareholder, and the passport must be valid. Every form then uses your name exactly as it appears in the passport.

For many of our formations, including US LLCs and UK companies, your passport and your details are all you send at the start.

2. Your details, exactly as on your documents

Alongside the passport, expect to give:

  • Full name as in the passport.
  • Nationality and date of birth.
  • Passport number.
  • Full residential address: house number, street, city, postcode and country.
  • Who owns what: each shareholder's percentage, adding up to 100%.

An incomplete address is one of the most common reasons we have to go back to a client. Write it the way it would appear on an official letter sent to you.

3. Identity checks

Registries and service providers now check identity before they act. In the UK, since 18 November 2025, every new director and every person with significant control must verify their identity with Companies House online, using a passport, before the company can be filed. For a US LLC, we check your identity before we file with the state.

These checks are quick when your documents are clear and match the details you gave.

4. Extra documents in some countries

Some countries need more:

  • Netherlands: a BV is set up through a Dutch notary, so passport copies must be certified with an apostille. You can sign remotely by power of attorney.
  • Saudi Arabia: the Saudi company is owned by a foreign company, so you need that company's audited financial statements, its documents attested by the Saudi embassy, a legal power of attorney and the investor's passport copy.
  • Pakistan: to take over an established company, non-Pakistanis provide a passport and an attested power of attorney. Pakistanis provide their national ID card (CNIC) and the mobile number registered to it.
  • Malaysia: a foreign-owned Sdn. Bhd. needs at least one director who lives in Malaysia. If you don't have one, we can quote a resident director service.

5. After formation: banks and payment providers

Once the company exists, bank and payment applications bring their own checks (KYC and KYB). Providers usually ask for the company's formation documents, a passport for each director and main owner (often with a selfie or video check), your residential address and a clear description of what the business sells. Each provider decides its own applications, and no one can guarantee approval. Our guide on why Stripe or a bank may reject a new company explains how to prepare.

Tips to avoid delays

  • Send clear colour scans, not angled photos with glare.
  • Use the same spelling of your name and address on every form.
  • Tell us early if a partner will join, so their documents are ready too.
  • Ask before you pay for translations or attestation: not every country needs them.

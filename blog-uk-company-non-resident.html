Opening a UK company without living in the UK: 5 things to know in 2026

10 Oct 2026 · 3 min read

A UK Ltd is one of the easiest companies to run from abroad, but the rules changed in 2025 and 2026. Here's what non-UK founders need to know before registering.

The UK private limited company (Ltd) is popular with founders around the world. It's quick to register, respected by customers, suppliers and marketplaces, and you can run it fully from abroad. Two recent changes matter, though: compulsory identity checks for directors, and higher Companies House fees. Here are the five things to know before you start.

1. You don't need to live in the UK

  • Directors and shareholders can be any nationality and live anywhere.
  • The company needs at least one director who is a real person, not only another company.
  • The company must always have a UK registered office address where official letters can be delivered. It appears on the public register, so many founders use a service address to keep their home address private.

2. Identity checks are now compulsory

Since 18 November 2025, every new director and every person with significant control (usually anyone owning more than 25%) must verify their identity with Companies House. It's done online with a passport, and it has to be completed before the company can be filed. Existing companies are moving to the new rules too, usually at their next confirmation statement.

3. It's fast once identity checks are done

  • Companies House: usually registers the company within 24 hours of filing.
  • Corporation Tax reference (UTR): HMRC usually sends it within 2 to 4 weeks.
  • Bank and payment accounts: timing depends on each provider. Approval is always their decision, based on their own checks.

4. Government fees went up in 2026

From 1 February 2026, registering a company online with Companies House costs £100, and the yearly confirmation statement costs £50. Check whether any quote you receive includes these fees. 5. Know your yearly duties

  • Confirmation statement at Companies House, once a year.
  • Annual accounts, due 9 months after the end of your financial year (the first ones can have a longer deadline).
  • Company Tax Return to HMRC, with Corporation Tax on profits, currently between 19% and 25%.
  • VAT registration if your sales pass the threshold or your business model requires it.
  • A UK registered office at all times.

Missing a deadline leads to automatic penalties, and a company that stops filing can be struck off the register.

Two things a UK company doesn't do

  • It doesn't give you a visa, residence or the right to work in the UK. Immigration is a separate process.
  • It doesn't guarantee a bank, Stripe or marketplace account. Each provider decides for itself.

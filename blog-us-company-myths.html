5 myths about owning a US company from abroad

23 Oct 2026 · 3 min read

"A US company gives me a US visa" is the myth we hear most often, but it isn't the only one. Here are five common beliefs about US companies for non-residents, and what is actually true.

A US LLC is one of the most popular ways for founders outside America to sell to US customers, open a US marketplace account or invoice clients in dollars. Because it is so popular, a lot of half-true advice circulates online. Believing it can lead to wrong expectations, wasted money or missed deadlines.

Here are five myths we hear from clients, and the facts behind each one.

Myth 1: "A US company gives me a US visa"

It doesn't. Forming a US company does not give you a visa, residency or the right to work in the United States. Your company can trade with US customers while you live and work in your own country, but immigration is a completely separate process with its own rules, decided by the US authorities.

If anyone tells you that registering an LLC is a route to living in the US, be careful. Treat the company as a business tool, not an immigration plan.

Myth 2: "I have to live in the US, or at least visit, to open one"

Not true. Non-residents can own 100% of a US LLC, alone or with partners. You don't need to travel to the US, and you don't need an SSN or ITIN to get an EIN, the company's US tax ID. The whole process, from filing with the state to the EIN application, can be done remotely.

Myth 3: "Once I have a company, Stripe or a bank will approve me"

A US company is usually one of the requirements, but it is not an approval. Banks and payment providers such as Wise, Payoneer and Stripe run their own checks on the owner, the business activity, the countries involved and the website. Each provider makes its own decision, and no one can guarantee it.

What you can do is prepare well: consistent documents, a clear business activity and a working website. Our guide on why Stripe or a bank may reject a new company explains how.

Myth 4: "A US company means I pay no tax"

A US company does not decide your taxes on its own. What you owe depends on your activity, where you live and where your customers are. Your home country may also have rules about income earned through a foreign company.

There are also filing duties whatever you earn: every foreign-owned LLC must file Form 5472 each year. For advice on your own situation, speak to a qualified tax professional. We refer clients to one when needed.

Myth 5: "After I form it, there is nothing else to do"

Forming the company is the start, not the end. To keep a US LLC in good standing you need, each year:

  • A registered agent in your state, to receive official mail and legal notices.
  • The state annual report, where your state requires one.
  • Form 5472 with a pro-forma 1120, filed with the IRS for foreign-owned single-member LLCs.

Delaware companies also pay a yearly state tax. Missing these duties can lead to penalties or the company losing its good standing.

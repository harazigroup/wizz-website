Wyoming, New Mexico or Delaware: choosing the state for your US LLC

9 Nov 2026 · 3 min read

A US LLC is formed in one state, and you don't need to live there. For most non-US founders the choice comes down to three states. Here is how they compare.

When you form a US LLC, you form it in one state. You don't have to live in that state, visit it or sell only to customers there. Your LLC can sell across the US and to the rest of the world. What the state mainly decides is your yearly paperwork, your running cost and how others see your company.

For most non-US founders the choice comes down to three states: Wyoming, New Mexico and Delaware.

Wyoming: our default

Wyoming is the state we use unless you ask for another. It has low yearly costs and strong privacy for owners. It asks for an annual report each year, which keeps your company in good standing. It is a solid all-round choice for e-commerce sellers, agencies and online service businesses.

New Mexico: the lowest running cost

New Mexico has no annual report, so it has the lowest running cost of the three. It fits founders who want to keep yearly costs to a minimum and run a simple business. Your LLC still needs a registered agent in the state at all times, and the federal filings to the IRS still apply.

Delaware: best known with investors

Delaware is the name most investors recognise. Startups that plan to raise money often form there, usually as a C-Corp rather than an LLC. It costs more to run: Delaware charges a USD 400 yearly tax from 2026. We form Delaware companies on request, and it adds USD 100 to the package. If you are planning a C-Corp, we prepare a written quote.

What the state does not change

  • Federal filings. A foreign-owned single-member LLC files Form 5472 with a pro-forma Form 1120 each year, whichever state it is in.
  • Your EIN. We apply to the IRS for your EIN the same way in every state. You don't need an SSN or ITIN.
  • Bank and payment approvals. The state doesn't decide whether a bank, Wise, Payoneer or Stripe accepts your company. Each provider makes its own decision after its own checks.
  • Your personal tax. What you pay, and where, depends on where you live and how you run the business. For that, speak to a qualified tax professional in your country.

A quick way to choose

  • Selling online and want a simple, low-cost setup with privacy: Wyoming.
  • Want the lowest yearly running cost: New Mexico.
  • Planning to raise money from investors: Delaware, usually as a C-Corp.

If you plan to open an office or hire staff in a particular US state, tell us at the start, as that state may have its own registration rules.
